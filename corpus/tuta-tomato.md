## Summary
Tuta absoluta was first found in Nepal by NARC's Entomology Division on 16 May 2016 on a commercial tomato farm in Balaju, Kathmandu. It survives year round at 20 to 27 °C, so the mid hills and the Terai can carry it except in winter. Larvae mine leaves, stems and fruit; without control, tomato yield can fall by 80 to 100% in open fields and plastic houses. Spinosad, chlorantraniliprole and novaluron are recommended in Nepal, but studies report weak results because the pest has developed resistance.

The Indian labels in Parkha's table list "leaf miner" on tomato only for Liriomyza, a different pest. Parkha does not have a verified dose for Tuta absoluta. Waiting periods on tomato still apply to any product used.

## Steps
- Hang pheromone traps for mass trapping, at the spacing the trap supplier gives.
- Pick and destroy mined leaves and damaged fruit; do not leave them in the field.
- Remove crop residue after harvest and do not follow tomato with potato, brinjal or chilli.
- If you spray, do not use the same product twice in a row; resistance builds fast.
- Ask the Agriculture Knowledge Centre which product is registered for Tuta absoluta and follow its waiting period.
